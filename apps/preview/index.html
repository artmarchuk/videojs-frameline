    What Studio checks

    Six things other players leave out. Each one has a key, and each row does it in the player above.

    1. IO

      A range that plays only itself.

      Mark In and Out and playback stays inside: it starts at In and loops. R turns the loop off; then it stops on Out.

    2. ,.

      One frame at a time, in timecode.

      HH:MM:SS:FF with a start offset and drop-frame counting. Frame-accurate where the browser allows; where it can’t be, the timecode’s tooltip says so.

    3. S

      Point at an exact moment.

      The host’s markers show as pins on the ruler and in the Markers menu. S copies a link to the frame on screen, as timecode. Opening the link lands on that frame, paused.

    4. ShiftV

      Guides for delivery.

      Title and action safe, thirds, centre, crops from 9:16 to 2.39:1, and the interface zones of YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok, labelled approximate.

    5. Z

      Zoom that is true to the source.

      At 100%, one pixel of the best rendition is one device pixel. When a lower rendition is playing, the skin says so.

    6. G

      A frame grab is the frame as encoded.

      G saves the frame on screen as a PNG, at the video’s own size and with no guides in it. Studio draws around the picture and never changes its pixels.

    7. Shift?

      Every action has a key, is announced to screen readers and is listed in the help dialog. Space, K, J, L and F work as they do on YouTube, in both tiers. Built to WCAG 2.2 AA; the automated axe audit finds zero violations in six states.

    Host

    Built to be hosted

    One skin, two tiers. Studio is for the people who make the video. Their audience gets Viewer, and downloads none of the Studio code.

    Data in
    The host supplies markers and chapters; the skin draws them.
    Events out
    Range, marker, frame, guides, zoom, frame grab, copy link.
    A handle
    apiRef drives the tools from the host’s own UI.
    Flags
    Switch a single tool on over Viewer, or off over Studio.
    Slots, tokens
    The host’s own buttons, the host’s own colours.

    Out of frame

    What Frameline won’t do

    Studio adds controls and overlays around the picture. These three stay outside the frame line, on purpose.

    • No image processing.

      No channels, exposure, gamma or scopes. A skin is not a grading engine, and a check is only honest on pixels nobody changed.

    • No setting posters.

      Choosing a poster needs a back end. That is the host product’s job; the skin hands it the frame.

    • No light theme for the player.

      A neutral dark surround keeps judgements of exposure and contrast honest.
